_What are your services? How well are they performing? How much do they cost?_ 

When organisations start down the road of trying to manage their services better, often the initial focus is on building agreement on the scope of each service, its outcomes, its design and its owner.

But having visibility of the cost of each service is just as important - and should be in that top list as well.

Organisations typically seek to identify an owner of each service - an individual who can make tradeoffs between competing priorities on the service and oversee its design and performance.

In my experience, that responsibility is only meaningful if they operate within known constraints. And the expenditure envelope is one of the most important constraints.


# Working out service cost

Budgets are usually organised around functions, not services. So for example digital costs will come from Digital/Data budgets, Legal costs will come from Legal budgets, communications costs will come from Comms budgets etc.

The costs of the whole service are therefore not naturally transparent. There needs to be work done in conjunction with Finance teams to allocate a fair proportion of expenditure from these functional budgets to the service.

There are lots of ways to do this - e.g. it could be based on proportion of staff time or some other usage rate. The key is to start small and simple and then make it more refined as needed over time.

There may be calls for some shared services costs (e.g. property, HR, etc) to simply be ‘top-sliced’ which means taking a set percentage aside for them as a kind of levy. In my opinion this should be resisted - the point is to show all of the costs and not unduly shelter any from scrutiny.

As is often the case, the exercise of working this out - and the conversations around it - are often as valuable as the final calculation. With one organisation I worked with, costing conversations quickly led to a discussion about whether functional staff should be ‘ringfenced’ to the service (which might mean closer collaboration and fewer handoffs) or whether they must continue to sit in functional teams. It’s hard to have this conversation without the numbers.
